1. Objective and Hypothesis

Standard slow sand filters are highly effective at reducing turbidity, but struggle against smaller viral loads and long-term bacterial breakthrough. Our hypothesis was that coating the upper active biological layer (schmutzdecke) with silver-doped titanium dioxide nanoparticles would drastically increase pathogen elimination without slowing down flow rates.

2. Experimental Setup

We deployed two identical columns under controlled indoor laboratory conditions. Column A acted as the baseline control (pure silica sand), while Column B integrated the nanoparticle coating in the top 5cm media layer. Both systems were fed standard municipal water spiked with regulated non-harmful bacterial surrogates.

Parameter Control Column (A) Nanoparticle Column (B) Improvement
E. Coli Reduction 98.2% 99.99% +1.79%
Average Flow Rate 0.4 L/min 0.38 L/min -5% (Negligible)
Turbidity Output < 0.5 NTU < 0.3 NTU Clearer outflow

4. Conclusion & Next Steps

The experiment successfully validated our nanoparticle coating technique, yielding significantly higher pathogen reduction without clogging or compromising flow speed. For our next phase, we will scale up the prototype for field testing in low-resource community centers over a 6-month seasonal cycle.
